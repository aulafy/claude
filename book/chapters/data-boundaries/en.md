## Before you start

Your goal is still to draft a notice, not to connect AI to your email or grant access to documents. You will decide which information it actually needs to help.

Use only the fictional workshop from the previous chapters. Do not paste other people's conversations, passwords or files.

## 1. Separate the task from the file

Imagine a conversation containing the workshop details and unrelated messages. To improve the wording, you only need the activity, time, room and materials. You do not need to share the whole conversation to supply those four facts.

First describe the task: “draft a notice from these facts”. If you later need more information, decide why the task needs it before adding it.

## 2. Prepare a practice version

Use this fictional brief for the exercise:

```text
Activity: writing workshop.
Time: Saturday, 10:00 to 11:00.
Location: room 2.
Materials: a notebook.
Task: draft a short notice without sending it.
Unnecessary data: names, phone numbers, email addresses, passwords.
```

That is enough to practise writing. Replacing a name in a real document does not guarantee that the remaining details cannot identify someone. Do not treat that change as automatic permission to share the document.

## 3. Separate information from permissions

Providing four facts for a draft is not the same as allowing access to email or authorising delivery. The tool needs no access to accounts or files for this exercise.

If an interface suggests connecting services, that is a separate decision. You do not need it to finish this activity. Stop that step and return to the text example without granting extra permissions.

## 4. Choose where the work stops

Your stopping point is a reviewed draft. You decide what happens to it afterwards. Do not paste it into a real communication channel as part of this exercise.

Record the decision in one sentence:

> I used fictional information to prepare a draft. I did not share private files or authorise delivery.

If you work in an organisation, find out which tools and uses are authorised before using real information. This exercise does not replace your organisation's rules or a privacy review.

## Your result

Keep the fictional brief and check three things: the details relate to the task, you added no personal information and the result is still only a draft.

You have completed the first sequence of the book: **request, check and limit**. The proposed next part will apply the same cycle to longer documents. While it is being prepared, repeat these three chapters with another entirely fictional notice and see whether you need less help.
