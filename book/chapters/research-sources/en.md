## Before you start

Research is not collecting links. It means answering a question while showing where each detail came from and what remains unchecked. In this practice you will use a fictional topic so you do not depend on current information.

## 1. Ask a checkable question

Start with this question:

> What does a person need to take part in the writing workshop?

Break it into small claims: schedule, location, materials and registration. A broad question creates answers that are hard to review; a concrete list makes gaps visible.

## 2. Classify the sources

Imagine that you find these three pieces:

| Piece | Type | Proper use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organizer's official note | Primary source | Check what is announced |
| Magazine article | Secondary commentary | Guide questions and context |
| AI answer | Drafting help | Suggest terms, never prove a fact |

The label does not guarantee correctness. Read the specific source, its date and its scope. An official page can still be outdated or refer to another workshop.

## 3. Make a minimal record

For each claim, write one row:

```text
Claim: participants should bring a notebook.
Source: organizer's official note.
Location: paragraph about materials.
Status: supported in this exercise.
Checked on: the date I reviewed it.
```

Do not invent a URL, publication date or page number. If you cannot locate the claim in the source, write “pending”.

## 4. Write without hiding uncertainty

A traceable summary could say:

> The official note says that the workshop takes place on Saturday in room 2 and that participants should bring a notebook. The article agrees with those details, but it is used only as context. None of the pieces explains how to confirm attendance.

Notice the difference between “the note says” and “the AI says”. The first points to a reviewable source. The second describes where a suggestion came from, not whether it is valid.

When sources disagree, keep both versions, explain the disagreement and ask the responsible person when the detail matters. Do not choose the most recent version only because it sounds more convincing.

## Your result

Keep the divided question, the classification, the claim record and the summary with visible uncertainties. You are done when another person can repeat your path and find the same support.

For legal, medical, financial or security matters, this is only an organizing technique: use sources and review appropriate to the risk. In the next chapter you will compare tools with a small, repeatable test.
