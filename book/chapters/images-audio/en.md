## Before you start

You will plan a fictional media piece, but you will not publish it. A tool's output does not remove human decisions about the brief, references, consent, privacy and licensing.

For practice, imagine a poster for a neighborhood library. Do not use photographs of real people or recognizable voices.

## 1. Write the brief

Record the purpose, audience, format and elements that must appear. For example: “A vertical poster for a children's reading afternoon. It should be clear and welcoming, with space for the date and place”.

Also record what must not appear: children's names, identifiable faces, real phone numbers, someone else's logos or unconfirmed claims.

## 2. Separate the references

A reference can be an image, audio, palette, description or work you found. Record where it came from and what permission you have to use it. “It is on the internet” does not mean “it can be reused”.

If you cannot justify the license, replace it with your own description or a resource with clear permission. Keep that decision with the project.

## 3. Generate a first version

Request a composition, not a copy of a particular work. Include the format and constraints of the brief. If the tool stores the prompt or settings, archive them with the date and version identifier.

For audio, add the language, approximate duration and tone. Do not use a real person's voice as a reference without explicit consent and permitted use.

## 4. Review before sharing

Check four layers:

1. **Content**: does it meet the brief and avoid invented facts?
2. **People**: does it show or imitate anyone identifiable without permission?
3. **Materials**: can you explain the license for every reference?
4. **Accessibility**: is the text readable, does contrast work and does audio have a transcript or alternative?

Record problems and keep the reviewed version. Do not present a generated piece as a documentary photograph.

## 5. Spot the common failure

An image may introduce unreadable text, distorted hands, symbols resembling brands or a scene that looks real although it is invented. Audio may mispronounce a name or attribute a sentence to someone.

If the error changes the meaning, stop publication. Crop, regenerate or change technique. When context matters, label the piece clearly as generated or composited.

## Your result

You have prepared a minimum usage record: brief, references, permissions, settings, review and accessible alternative. This record lets you correct the piece and answer questions without relying on memory.
