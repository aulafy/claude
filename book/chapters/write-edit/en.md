## Before you start

You will request a draft from a short source and then edit it. The goal is not to make it sound convincing: every sentence should be classifiable as a source fact, a recognized omission or a suggestion.

Work with this fictional text:

```text
The book club meets on Thursday at 18:00 in room 1.
The session lasts 30 minutes. Each person may bring a book.
The text does not state a price, number of places or registration method.
```

## 1. Separate what you know

Before writing, extract a list of facts: Thursday at 18:00, room 1, 30 minutes and the option to bring a book. Add another list of omissions: price, number of places and registration method.

Do not turn an omission into a “probably”. A person reading the notice must be able to distinguish confirmed information from what still needs an answer.

## 2. Request a bounded draft

A useful request could be:

> Write a short notice using only the facts in the source. Include a “To confirm” section with the missing details. Do not invent a price, number of places or registration method.

The word “only” does not guarantee a correct answer, but it makes the criterion for your review visible.

## 3. Review sentence by sentence

Label each sentence in the draft as **source**, **omission** or **suggestion**. Delete or rewrite any detail you cannot find in the starting text. If you need to add a call to action, write it as a suggestion, not a fact.

An acceptable result could say: “The club meets on Thursday at 18:00 in room 1. The session lasts 30 minutes and you may bring a book. The price, number of places and registration method still need confirmation.”

## 4. Spot the common failure

A draft may add “free entry”, “limited capacity” or “reserve your place” because these are common phrases. They are plausible details, but the source does not support them. Plausible does not mean true.

When you find one, keep the original source in your notes, remove the unsupported statement and record the question that still needs an answer. This improves the message without fabricating information.

## Your result

You have created a traceable draft: you know what comes from the source, what is missing and what a person must confirm. This pattern works for notices, emails, summaries and help pages.
