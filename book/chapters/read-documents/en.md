## Before you start

In the previous chapters you learned to limit a task and check an answer. Now you will apply the same cycle to a short document. You do not need to upload a file to an AI tool: use the fictional text in this lesson or copy it into a tool you already use.

By the end you will have a short summary, a list of unanswered questions and a check against the original document.

## 1. Keep the original document

Use this fictional note as your only source:

```text
Library team meeting
The meeting will be on Tuesday, April 14 at 4:00 p.m. in the blue room.
Each person may bring one activity example to discuss.
The expected duration is 45 minutes.
The document does not say who will lead the meeting or how attendance will be confirmed.
```

Do not add names, email addresses or real files. Keep the original text available while checking any summary.

## 2. Extract facts and questions

Before summarizing, turn the note into a table. This separation keeps an unanswered question from becoming a disguised fact:

| Item | What the source says |
| --- | --- |
| Date and time | Tuesday, April 14, 4:00 p.m. |
| Location | Blue room |
| Preparation | Bringing an activity example is optional |
| Duration | 45 minutes expected |
| Unanswered | Who leads it and how attendance is confirmed |

The word “expected” matters: it does not guarantee that the meeting will last exactly 45 minutes. Keep that qualification in the summary.

## 3. Request a bounded summary

You can use this request in an AI chat or do it manually:

```text
Summarize the document in no more than five lines.
Use only facts from the text.
List the questions the document does not answer at the end.
Do not invent attendees, confirmation instructions or a leader.
Keep the word “expected” when describing the duration.
```

The request names a source, a size and boundaries. If you use a tool, paste only the fictional text from this practice.

## 4. Check the summary

Check every sentence against the document, not against memory. An acceptable result could be:

> The library team meeting will be on Tuesday, April 14 at 4:00 p.m. in the blue room. It is expected to last 45 minutes. People may bring an activity example to discuss. The document does not say who will lead the meeting or how to confirm attendance.

Look for three possible errors: a changed date, an invented obligation based on an option and an invented answer to an unanswered question. If you find one, correct it and check the complete summary again.

## Your result

Keep the original document, the facts-and-questions table and the final summary. You are done when you can point to the source for every summary sentence and identify what remains unanswered.

If someone needs a decision the document does not contain, do not ask the AI to guess. Ask the responsible person. In the next chapter you will practise comparing sources and deciding which one deserves more trust.
