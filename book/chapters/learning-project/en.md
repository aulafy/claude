## Before you start

You will design a short study session with help from AI. AI can ask questions, offer hints and point out mistakes; you must attempt, explain and decide.

Choose a fictional topic or one you can practise without sharing private data.

## 1. Define a demonstration

Complete this sentence: “By the end I can explain ___ and solve ___ without looking at the answer”. An observable goal is better than “understand everything”.

Break the topic into an opening question, your own attempt and a check. If you cannot make an attempt, start with a very small example.

## 2. Request graduated help

You can ask: “Do not solve the problem yet. Ask me one question that checks the concept. Wait for my answer. If I am wrong, give me a short hint and explain why; offer the complete solution only after my second attempt”.

This request protects space for thinking. It does not make AI an authority: its hints also need review.

## 3. Check your explanation

After solving the exercise, close the conversation and explain it in your own words. Add a new example and a case where the method does not work. If you can only repeat the AI's answer, you need more practice.

Keep the question, your first attempt, the hint and your final explanation. That sequence is more useful learning evidence than a perfect answer without a process.

## 4. Spot the common failure

A very detailed answer can make you feel that you learned even when you made no decisions. It can also correct you confidently and be wrong.

Compare the explanation with your study material or a suitable source. Mark doubts and ask about them. Do not submit text as your own if you cannot defend it.

## Your result

You have created a learning cycle: goal, attempt, hint, review and your own explanation. You can repeat it for different topics while keeping responsibility for what you know.
