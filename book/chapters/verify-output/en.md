## Before you start

In the previous chapter you prepared a notice. Now review one as if somebody else handed it to you. You do not need to consult AI: the example contains deliberate mistakes and the evidence is in front of you.

Your result will be a table separating confirmed facts, incorrect statements and things the supplied information cannot establish.

## 1. Keep the source

This is the original notice for the exercise:

> Writing workshop. Saturday, 10:00 to 11:00. Room 2. Bring a notebook. No price is stated.

Keep it beside any answer you review. An answer cannot serve as its own source.

## 2. Identify the claims

This draft contains deliberately introduced mistakes:

> The free writing workshop is on Saturday from 10:00 to 12:00 in room 2. Bring a notebook. Five places remain and you must confirm by Friday.

Before reading on, underline what you would check. Break the text into small claims: price, time, room, materials, places and deadline.

## 3. Compare each detail

| Claim | Check result | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It is free | Not verifiable from this source | No price is stated |
| It ends at 12:00 | Incorrect | The source says 11:00 |
| It is in room 2 | Confirmed | Matches the notice |
| Bring a notebook | Confirmed | Matches the notice |
| Five places remain | Not verifiable from this source | No capacity information |
| Confirm by Friday | Not verifiable from this source | No deadline is stated |

“Not verifiable” does not necessarily mean false. It means you lack a basis for presenting the claim as confirmed. For this exercise, remove unsupported information. If it were essential, you would need to ask the workshop organiser.

## 4. Rewrite without adding facts

Write a version containing only the original details, then check it again. You can ask for help correcting the draft, but the reference remains the original notice.

A common mistake is asking another AI whether the draft is correct without giving it the source. Agreement does not replace checking. An automated comparison may help, but you still need to inspect the evidence it uses.

## Your result

You have completed the activity when you can show the original notice, the comparison table and the corrected text. You do not need a longer answer or a more powerful tool.

- Which detail contradicted the source?
- Which information was simply absent?
- What did you change, and why?

This method works for a draft, summary or requirements list. For consequential tasks, this kind of check may be insufficient: review must fit the context. For now, practise with the fictional example.
